Biopsychosocial and Epigenetic Markers of Heat-Stress and Posttraumatic Outcomes in Emergency Responders

Paul Ruiz Pinto, Mackenzie Rubens, Dagmar Bruenig, Jane Shakespeare-Finch, Caroline Nievergelt, Marcus Ising and Divya Mehta.

Study and analysis

Emergency responder professions differ in their physical and psychosocial exposures. We examined whether epigenetic ageing trajectories differed between professions and explored methylation-associated biological processes using a heat-response gene reference set.

Baseline dataset: 332 participants; 168 paramedics, 99 emergency medical dispatchers and 65 firefighters.

Psychosocial assessments and saliva-derived DNA methylation were examined across two timepoints approximately 12 months apart. Methylation was measured using Illumina MethylationEPIC v2 arrays.

Epigenetic age acceleration was assessed using Horvath, Hannum, PhenoAge and GrimAge clocks. Longitudinal models included participant random intercepts and adjusted for sex, ethnicity, trauma exposure, BMI and estimated cell composition.

Hannum change over time

The Timepoint × Profession interaction tested whether changes from T1 to T2 differed by profession, using paramedics as the reference group.

Among the four clocks, Hannum showed evidence of profession-related differences in change over time (Timepoint × Profession). Firefighters showed a greater decline in Hannum age acceleration between T1 and T2 than paramedics. The change among emergency medical dispatchers did not differ significantly from that among paramedics.

Firefighter difference in T1-to-T2 change relative to paramedics: -2.3264 years (SE 0.5248, nominal p = 0.0000166). Dispatcher difference: +0.1485 years (SE 0.4567, nominal p = 0.745). Mixed-effects model: 464 observations from 330 participants.

At baseline, firefighters had higher PhenoAge acceleration than paramedics: β = 1.52 years, SE = 0.76, nominal p = 0.047.

Biological processes

Interpretation

The decline could reflect changes in immune-related processes, including recovery from acute immune activation. Firefighting involves exposures such as heat, smoke, disrupted sleep and physical exertion. If T1 coincided with recent exposure and T2 with relative recovery, this could contribute to the observed pattern.

Blood-based research shows that Hannum estimates are sensitive to immune cell composition. Immune recovery therefore remains a possible explanation, rather than an established mechanism in this saliva-based study. The decline does not demonstrate reversal of biological ageing. [4]

Psychological symptoms and positive factors

Psychological symptoms and positive psychological factors showed no consistent pattern of association across the four epigenetic clocks. Symptoms were assessed approximately 12 months apart - a relatively short interval over which changes in DNAm-based ageing may be subtle, potentially limiting detection of associations. Technical variability can also complicate the interpretation of small longitudinal changes. [3]

Next step

Longer follow-up combining exposure measurements, inflammatory markers and repeated DNA methylation assessments could help distinguish transient occupational responses from sustained changes in epigenetic ageing.
